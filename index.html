Chapter 1: Where I Come From

I was born in Hong Kong in 2005. I grew up in a small, densely populated city, surrounded by overworked office workers during the day and bustling nightlife after dusk. I was raised alongside my older sister, and our childhood was shaped by a blend of Chinese and Western influences. We celebrated both Chinese New Year and Christmas, and grew up enjoying Cantonese cuisine.

Every two to three years, my family would buy four flight tickets to Canada to visit my maternal relatives. My grandparents lived in the suburbs, and I distinctly remember stepping outside to breathe in the fresh air and look up at the wide, blue sky. I also remember my grandparents standing at the front door of their big house, waiting to welcome us. My grandfather and his family immigrated to Canada during the 1990s, a period when many Hong Kong residents were emigrating ahead of the 1997 Handover of Hong Kong from British to Chinese rule. His decision to come here became the beginning of my family's connection to the country.

Although my mother eventually returned to Hong Kong with my father, where they settled down and raised my sister and me, Canada remained a part of our family history.

Growing up, I never thought much about why my family had moved between these two places. To me, Hong Kong was home, while Canada was somewhere I visited every few years to see my grandparents. I never imagined that one day, I would make the same journey as my grandfather, leaving Hong Kong to build a life of my own in Canada.
